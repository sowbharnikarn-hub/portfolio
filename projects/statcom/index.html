STATCOM Based Power Quality Improvement

For Grid-Connected Wind-Energy Systems — an Innovative Renewable Energy Program

A fuzzy-logic-controlled STATCOM that stabilises voltage and improves power quality where intermittent wind power meets the grid — built around an ATMEL ATmega8 microcontroller, PWM switching, and an H-Bridge inverter.

I.

Algorithms Employed

Two techniques work together: one decides how much compensation is needed, the other decides how to physically deliver it.

i. Control Algorithm

Fuzzy Logic Controller (FLC)

Used in place of — or alongside — a conventional PI controller, to give more robust, adaptive control over the highly non-linear dynamics of a wind energy conversion system. It uses "if-then" rules to determine the reactive current needed for compensation, improving dynamic response during voltage sags, swells and load changes. Inputs — voltage error and its rate of change — pass through fuzzification, an inference engine, and defuzzification to produce the control output.

ii. Switching Technique

Pulse Width Modulation (PWM)

The modulation method used to generate switching signals for the voltage source converter (VSC). Reference currents from the control algorithm are compared against a carrier signal to produce precise gating signals for the inverter's IGBTs — rapid, precise switching that lets the STATCOM inject a current waveform which cancels harmonics and maintains unity power factor at the Point of Common Coupling (PCC).

II.

Main Components

i. Central Processor

ATMEL ATmega8 Microcontroller

The central processing unit for the whole control scheme — it executes the fuzzy control algorithm, processes sensor input measuring voltage and current at the PCC, and generates the high-frequency PWM signals that drive the H-Bridge inverter. Its role is critical for real-time STATCOM operation.

ii. Power Stage

H-Bridge Inverter

The STATCOM's voltage source converter is built from H-Bridge circuits per phase. Each bridge uses switching devices (IGBTs) to create an AC output from a DC input — a DC-link capacitor. By adjusting the phase and magnitude of that output relative to the grid voltage via PWM, the STATCOM rapidly absorbs or injects reactive power, regulating terminal voltage and improving overall power quality.
