Company Background

A 160-year-old British house, repositioning for China

Burberry is a luxury fashion brand renowned for its unique British brand image. This British fashion brand was founded by Thomas Burberry in 1856 for clothing apparel, fragrances and accessories, with its first store opened in Basingstoke, UK. By 1867 they had established outdoor attire and developed their signature pattern — the trench check coat, patented in 1912 — for which they remain renowned. Burberry has grown into a famous international luxury brand, with global reach across 44 countries, employing over 10,000 staff and headquartered in London.

Over the past five years, £1.2bn has been returned to shareholders in dividends and buy-backs, while the company has maintained its dividend per share at or above the previous year's value. The new management team at Burberry — with Marco Gobbetti replacing Christopher Bailey as CEO in 2017 — has put forward a new capital allocation framework applied to boost shareholder value. The segmentation chosen for this study is narrowed to China, because the China market has marked tremendous growth in Burberry's revenue and sales over the past few years, which is in turn experiencing a slight decline recently (Burberry.com, 2018).

Key Issues

What's actually pressuring the business

−1%

Revenue Decline

FY2017/18 revenue slipped from £2,766M to £2,733M — despite mass improvement the year before, from £2,515M to £2,766M. A softer US market pushed shares down 8pc to £15.70, with a predicted £10M hit for 2018.

−16%

Wholesale Revenue

The decline in wholesale revenue has been offset only partly by retail growth of 3% — a structural shift the new strategy must account for.

⚔

Intense Competition

Though Burberry reflects a respectable luxury image, it has never attained the top-most position in the fashion industry.

↓

Market Share & Asset Utilisation

Market share decline sits alongside ineffective asset utilisation — capital and retail footprint not yet converting into proportional returns.
