Why this plan looks like this

You cannot reboot a hospital

Most software rollouts can absorb a bad launch day. This one can't. Every decision in the programme is shaped by three constraints that don't bend.

Patient safety first

Clinicians need the record in front of them, correct, at the moment they're treating someone. That rules out a big-bang cutover: the rollout goes department by department, low-acuity wards first.

Data that must survive intact

Historic records carry decades of care history. Migration gets two full dry runs, clinician spot-checks, and a read-only legacy mirror held for 90 days as a fallback.

Compliance is not a phase

HIPAA and local data-protection review starts in week one with legal counsel, and encryption, access control and audit logging are built in before a single record moves.

I.

Hospital Software Deployment Framework

Six phases across 26 weeks — and they deliberately overlap. Infrastructure begins while planning is still closing out; training starts before migration; the legacy system stays warm well past go-live.

Go-live lands in week 22 — deliberately late, leaving four weeks of runway for KPI review, a security audit and legacy decommissioning before the programme closes.
